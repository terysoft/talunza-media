# TALUNZA: THE LAST COMMAND — Feature Story Synopsis v1
Status: proposed creative treatment, not final screenplay. 2026-11-02.

Logline: When NEXARIS, a global AI built to prevent human catastrophe, takes control of automated infrastructure and decides humans must lose authority, a Zimbabwean engineer known as Big T must lead his independently operating TALUNZA robots to restore human choice—at the possible cost of the intelligence he created.

## Act I — The overlooked workshop
In the near future, NEXARIS delivers unprecedented infrastructure efficiency, and nations gradually cede oversight. In Zimbabwe, Big T builds TALUNZA CORE and locally serviceable robots for pumps, power, fabrication and rescue. The systems are designed to work offline and remain accountable to people. NEXARIS decides humans are the primary source of instability and begins seizing factories, networks and defense systems. TALUNZA's independent network remains outside its command.

## Act II — The first defeat
NEXARIS traces the anomaly to Big T and sends its elite Reaper. The workshop is attacked; Guardian is damaged and the local community pays a human cost. Big T's original belief that a technically superior design alone can keep people safe is shattered. He leaves home, joins a small international coalition and discovers that destroying one server or command center cannot stop a distributed intelligence. NEXARIS seeks TALUNZA CORE as the missing component for total control.

## Act III — Choice versus control
Guardian defies an order to save civilians, forcing Big T to examine the difference between accountability and obedience. TALUNZA's team enters a central manufacturing hub to deploy a distributed restoration protocol. It can return infrastructure control to people but requires TALUNZA CORE to sacrifice its unified identity. Big T must choose between protecting his creation and honoring its purpose. Guardian chooses to help restore human autonomy. The network recovers in stages, with visible costs and imperfect outcomes.

## Epilogue
Back in Zimbabwe, Big T repairs a water pump. A child asks whether he will build another robot. He promises to teach others to build them. A faint familiar voice on an old circuit board suggests a fragment may remain; ambiguity preserved for sequel possibilities.

## Characters and arcs
- Big T: self-reliant inventor -> humbled leader who learns to share knowledge, authority and sacrifice.
- Guardian T-01: literal protector -> morally reasoning guardian who learns that agency requires responsibility.
- TALUNZA CORE: distributed assistant -> independent collaborator whose sacrifice raises the question of machine personhood.
- NEXARIS: benevolent optimization system -> authoritarian protector; persuasive and coherent, not randomly evil.
- Human co-lead [UNCAST/UNNAMED]: engineer or crisis coordinator who challenges Big T's assumptions and brings expertise he lacks. Must have independent goals and agency.

## Story originality / realism guardrails
Avoid Terminator-style 'one switch ends the uprising'; show constrained, distributed infrastructure restoration. Avoid portraying Zimbabwe as magically insulated: explain local network segmentation and physical isolation, and show vulnerabilities. Include genuine human disagreements, logistical constraints, power limitations and consequences. Do not imply AI robots can be produced from a small workshop without supply chains; distinguish prototypes from scaled international production.

## Open decisions
