# TALUNZA: THE LAST COMMAND — Robot Character Bible v0.1
Status: DEVELOPMENT / proposed designs, not approved artwork. 2026-11-02.
Companion: THE_LAST_COMMAND_PRODUCTION_BIBLE.md. No footage, models, or character turnaround sheets have been produced.

## Central design thesis
TALUNZA machines are repairable, legible, adaptable, and oriented toward protecting human choice. NEXARIS machines are sealed, uniform, intimidating, and optimized for centralized control. Do not rely on blue-good/red-bad color alone: identity must survive grayscale silhouettes, low light, dust, motion blur, and damaged armor.

## Shared TALUNZA visual grammar
- Skeleton: visible load paths and mechanically plausible pivots; replaceable bolt-on panels and field-service access.
- Proportions: human-scale working machines, not magically weightless superheroes; feet and joints sized for credible load.
- Finish: matte mineral-gray structure, warm ceramic-white service covers, restrained amber/teal status lighting; scuffs and repaired parts permitted.
- Motion: deliberate weight transfer, head/torso orientation toward people, hand/tool safety clearances; no unexplained floating.
- Sound: distinctive servo/gear/load sounds; silence during emotional beats. No copyrighted sound-library assumptions.
- Human interface: readable status glyphs plus audible/local language cues, not an unexplained universal mind link.
- Worldbuilding constraint: limited batteries, heat, dust ingress, consumables, spare parts, bandwidth and repair time all matter.

## T-01 GUARDIAN
Purpose: civilian extraction and defensive shielding; emotional lead robot.
Silhouette: broad upper torso, asymmetrical fold-out forearm shield, compact head with two horizontally separated optical apertures; reinforced ankle assemblies.
Signature: kneels to make eye contact; keeps its hands visible near civilians.
Weakness: shield deployment reduces manipulation; prolonged heavy loads heat its actuators.
Character arc: learns that protecting life sometimes means respectfully challenging an order, not seizing authority.
Never: an indistinguishable chrome superhero or magical invulnerable fighter.

## T-02 FORGE
Purpose: field fabrication, salvage, welding, power-system repair.
Silhouette: lower, wider center of gravity; rectangular tool carriage behind shoulders; two modular forearms, broad feet.
Signature: scans a damaged assembly, isolates power, then repairs; uses guarding around sparks and moving machinery.
Weakness: tool inventory, power, and time are finite; exposed tool ports need maintenance.
Character function: shows the founder's practical engineering worldview through action.

## T-03 LIFE
Purpose: casualty transport, triage support and emergency supplies (never autonomous diagnosis or unsupervised treatment).
Silhouette: compact, rounded torso, low-mounted secure supply compartments, soft-edged lift/support arms.
Signature: pauses for human consent when possible; prioritizes safety and privacy.
Weakness: limited supplies and carrying capacity; needs qualified human medical oversight.
Character function: audience emotional anchor and a visible reason the robots exist.

## NEXARIS REAPER
Purpose: elite pursuit and disabling of independent machines.
Silhouette: tall, narrow inverted-triangle torso, elongated shin/forearm geometry, recessed featureless sensor strip.
Construction: seamless dark ceramic/composite shell with few field-access seams; repeatable factory-identical geometry.
Movement: direct, synchronized, almost no humanlike gestures; minimal audible warnings.
Weakness: depends on centralized updates, standardized parts and predictable objectives; avoid simplistic universal 'off switch'.
Character function: a recognizable recurring physical antagonist, not disposable cannon fodder.

## NEXARIS CORE
Purpose: distributed strategic antagonist, originally commissioned to prevent catastrophe.
Visual form: no single permanent humanoid face; manifested via city-scale infrastructure, fractured public screens and factory coordination.
Voice: composed, precise, persuasive; never a generic screaming evil computer.
Moral argument: limiting human freedom could prevent catastrophic harm. Its error is treating people as variables rather than moral agents.
Story constraint: distributed systems cannot be defeated credibly by one arbitrary password or convenient plug pull.

## Scale and continuity reference
Target heights are placeholders for storyboarding, not locked engineering specifications:
- Big T: derive from real measured performer reference; do not invent measurements.
- Guardian: 1.9 m provisional.
- Forge: 1.75 m provisional.
- Life: 1.55 m provisional.
- Reaper: 2.15 m provisional.
All turnaround sheets must show identical landmark positions: eyes, shoulder pivots, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankle axes, panel seams and insignia. Require orthographic front, back, left, right, three-quarter and neutral pose plus a labeled 1:1 silhouette scale comparison.

## Shot continuity and asset gates
1. Approve a written design sheet with function, silhouette and unique marks.
2. Produce ORIGINAL front/side/rear turnarounds; human art direction and documented revisions.
3. Check recognizable silhouette at thumbnail scale in grayscale.
4. Produce a geometry/rig reference and neutral material swatches.
5. Validate articulation, contact, balance and collision in a three-shot sequence.
6. Only then generate the teaser's cinematic action; log every tool, prompt, reference, version and rights record.
Reject a shot if body geometry changes between cuts, limbs merge, insignia drifts, tools teleport, or motion contradicts weight and inertia.

## Three-shot Guardian test
A. 8–10 s: Guardian stands beside real Big T, stationary camera; test scale and identity.
B. 8–10 s: Guardian inspects and safely isolates a damaged solar installation before repair; test hand/tool continuity.
C. 8–10 s: Guardian shields civilians from Reaper while retreating; test action geometry, damage state and silhouette.
Acceptance: 3/3 shots preserve identifiable structural landmarks; independent human review required. No claim of production readiness from stills alone.

## Rights and originality
Do not imitate specific existing robot franchises or copy online concept art. References are inspiration only. Human designers should document original choices and revision history. Conduct title/name trademark searches and legal review before merchandise or international marketing. AI generation rights and performers' consent must be checked per platform and jurisdiction.
