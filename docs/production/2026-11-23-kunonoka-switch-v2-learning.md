## 2. V1 inspected learnings (observations)

1. Character and wardrobe survived repeated extensions with usable continuity; full-body 9:16 framing preserved shoe visibility.
2. First 10s output mainly showed general dancehall bounce; wristwatch tap, crossed-arm X and specific step were inconsistent, **even after repeated, verbose prompts**.
3. Adding more instructions did not materially solve the identity of the signature move. This suggests simplifying choreography and/or reference-guided motion, not simply increasing prompt length.
4. Extend returned **cumulative** 10s → 20s → 30s → 40s outputs. Never append earlier clips to that cumulative file again; probe the actual duration.
5. Final output could be cut down to 39s and edited locally. Flow's original audio was removed before the approved music was mixed in. Branding was placed in deterministic post-production, rather than trusted to generative text.
6. The first version was a valuable proof of pipeline and an engaging visual test, **not** a validated teachable dance routine or proven viral growth engine.

## 3. V2 creative lock (proposal; subject to shot QA)

**Series:** THE KUNONOKA SWITCH — V2: THE CLOCK-X SWITCH  
**Interpretation:** Watch the time → reject giving up → make the move.  
**Length target:** 30 seconds, 9:16.  
**Construction:** one new Flow 10-second generation + two controlled 10-second extensions, one at a time after QA. Use *Omni Flash 1.2* for extensions. New shots must not splice in V1 footage.

**Reduce the routine to three large, clearly differentiated motions:**
- **CLOCK:** tap the opposite wrist twice while bouncing, with hands not occluded.
- **NO-QUIT X:** form a clear X using forearms in front of chest; hold briefly, open arms wide.
- **SWITCH:** one right-left step and a confident forward point. No aerobatics and no complex spinning.

**Environment:** one clean Lowveld/Zimbabwe courtyard; uncluttered contrasting pavement; same navy tracksuit and white sneakers; reliable full-body locked camera; no camera whip, crowd or drastic cuts until the final payoff. Keep feet visible and enough headroom for overlays.

**Timing outline:**
- 0–10 s = demonstrate simple, legible CLOCK → X → SWITCH, one full sequence, centered; no excessive complexity.
- 10–20 s = repeat same three recognizable anchors with bigger hip-hop/dancehall bounce and one small heel-toe variation; NO new signature.
- 20–30 s = repeat the signature at most once more, then final confident pose and clean space for challenge text added later.

**Song test:** use the existing founder-supplied track, sourced at 0:33–1:03. Local measured rolling mean RMS at 8 kHz mono identified 0:33 as the loudest 30s start window in the original file; *perceptual hook and beat alignment remain subject to actual listening and video QA*. **Branding/edit:** exact TALUNZA name and approved site/slogan in final deterministic edit; challenge text "CAN YOU DO THE CLOCK-X SWITCH?". No dialogue or music in generated Flow footage; replace its audio with the selected song, do not layer both tracks. Keep source/provenance and AI disclosure available for publishing.

## 6. Reusable media-learning principle

**Observe → simplify → generate one small shot → inspect gesture and identity → preserve good work → extend → soundtrack and graphics → check on phone → publish with rights → measure participation.**

For a dance challenge, recognisable choreography is the product; spectacle is supporting presentation. Measure real remakes, saves, shares and actual user participation, not only view counts. The 12–15-second teachable derivative should follow the accepted full master, never be faked from footage where the gesture isn't actually present.
